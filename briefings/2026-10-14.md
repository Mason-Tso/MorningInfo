GROWTH EQUITY 101: ID Finance's new round, mixing debt and equity in the same raise, is a good way to learn about liquidation preference: the order in which investors get paid back if a company is ever sold or wound down. Debt holders almost always sit at the front of the line, getting repaid before any equity investor sees a cent; among equity investors, growth funds typically hold preferred shares that come with their own preference over the common stock that employees and founders hold. That stacking order is why growth investors often care less about a company's total valuation than about exactly what kind of security they're buying into. Asking a growth investor where their check sits in a company's "capital stack" is a sharp coffee-chat question.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's inflation report is a good chance to learn the difference between "headline" and "core" CPI. Headline CPI counts the price of everything in a typical shopping basket, gas and groceries included, while core CPI strips out food and energy because those prices swing around for reasons that have nothing to do with the broader economy, like a hurricane or an oil shock. The Fed pays closer attention to core CPI for that reason, even though headline CPI is the number that makes the news. When oil prices are moving as much as they have lately, the gap between the two numbers can say a lot about whether inflation is genuinely getting worse or just reflecting one volatile input.
