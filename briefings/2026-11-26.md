GROWTH EQUITY 101: Sierra's and Torus's rounds are both primary capital: new shares sold straight to the company, the cash landing on its balance sheet to fund things like Torus's new factory. A secondary sale is different: existing shareholders sell shares to a new buyer, and the company sees none of the proceeds. Secondaries have gotten common at this stage because employees and early backers want cash without waiting for an IPO, like at Stripe and Anthropic this year. Asking whether a huge round was primary or partly secondary is a sharp way to learn if the money fuels growth or just cashes someone out.

LEARN ONE THING

Wednesday's market is a good way to learn market-cap weighting: why a single stock can drag down an entire index. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq weight each company by its total value, so giant stocks like Nvidia can pull the whole index down even while hundreds of smaller stocks rise, close to what happened as small and midcap stocks outperformed. That's why "stocks fell" can hide a market where most stocks did fine, and why traders watch which stocks actually drove a move.
