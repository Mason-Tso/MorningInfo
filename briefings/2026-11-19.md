GROWTH EQUITY 101: Mercor's jump to a $20 billion valuation is a good way to learn ARR [annual recurring revenue: the subscription or contract revenue a company can count on repeating each year] and why growth deals get priced as a multiple of it. Growth investors divide a software or AI company's price tag by its ARR to get a multiple, then ask whether that multiple is justified by how fast the revenue is growing and how sticky it is. A company growing as fast as Mercor commands a far higher multiple than a slow grower, which is exactly why its valuation doubled in about a year. In a coffee chat, asking "what multiple did that deal price at" is a fast way to sound like you understand how growth investors think about price.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's MSCI news is a good way to learn how being added to or dropped from a major stock index moves real money, even when nothing about the underlying business has changed. Huge pools of money sit in index funds that mechanically buy whatever is on a given index and sell whatever drops off it, regardless of whether anyone thinks the stock is a good investment. That is why Strategy and Metaplanet losing their spot on MSCI's list could trigger billions of dollars of selling that has nothing to do with how either company is performing. It is also why getting added to a major index, like the S&P 500, is such a big deal for a newly public company: it guarantees a wave of buying from funds that have no choice but to own it.
