GROWTH EQUITY 101: OpenAI's trajectory above is a good way to learn valuation step-ups: a new round pricing a company higher than its last one, the normal path for a fast grower, which is how OpenAI went from $157 billion to $852 billion to $1.25 trillion in two years. A down round is the rare opposite, new money coming in below the last round's price, a warning sign to investors. Thinking Machines' retreat from its $50 billion ask isn't quite a down round, since that number never closed, but the same forces are at work. Asking an investor whether a round was a step-up, flat, or down is a sharp way to learn how a business is really doing.

LEARN ONE THING

Anthropic's slipping timeline is a good chance to learn what a roadshow and bookbuilding are. During a roadshow, executives and bankers meet big investors over several days, pitching the stock and gauging demand at different prices, then build an order book tracking that interest to set a final price meant to leave just enough demand so the stock pops a little on day one. When investors sound skeptical before the roadshow even starts, as with Anthropic now, banks sometimes push back the date or lower the price range rather than risk a weak book.
