GROWTH EQUITY 101: Mirendil's round is a good way to learn liquidation preferences, the protection most growth and venture investors build into a deal. When a company is later sold or wound down, preferred shares with a liquidation preference get paid back before common stockholders see a dime, usually the full amount invested before anyone else splits what's left. A plain "1x non-participating" preference just returns the investor's money first; richer versions let the investor take that payout and still share in the upside on top. The bigger and more uncertain the check, like Kleiner Perkins and Clearlake putting $1 billion into a company that discloses almost no revenue, the more those terms matter.

LEARN ONE THING

Anthropic's IPO is a good way to learn what a private placement is, since Nvidia, Google, Microsoft and other tech giants are buying $26 billion of its stock this way instead of through the public offering. A private placement lets a company sell shares directly to a chosen investor at the IPO price, outside the order book other investors bid into. Companies like it because it locks in a stable shareholder and signals confidence to everyone else, while the investor gets guaranteed access to a hot deal without competing for an allocation.
