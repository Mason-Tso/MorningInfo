GROWTH EQUITY 101: This week's two biggest stories are a good pair for learning the difference between primary and secondary shares. OpenAI's round is primary: new investor money flows directly into the company's bank account to fund growth. Anduril's tender is secondary: existing shareholders, in this case employees, are simply selling shares they already own to new buyers, so the company itself doesn't see a dollar of it. Both get called "funding news," but only one actually funds anything; asking an investor whether a deal was primary or secondary is a fast way to learn whether a company actually needed, or got, fresh cash.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's Nasdaq news is a good chance to learn the difference between a "correction" and a "bear market," two terms that get thrown around loosely but mean specific things on a trading desk. A correction is a drop of 10 percent or more from a recent high, which is what the Nasdaq hit Thursday measured from its record last month; a bear market is a drop of 20 percent or more, a much bigger and scarier move. Most corrections don't turn into bear markets, which is why traders use the softer word first and watch closely before reaching for the scarier one.
