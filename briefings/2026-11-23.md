GROWTH EQUITY 101: Fractile's round is a good way to learn why growth and venture investors take minority stakes instead of buying a company outright. Thrive, Founders Fund and the other backers own a slice of Fractile and a board seat, but the founders keep control and run it day to day, unlike a buyout firm that buys most of a company and runs it directly. Investors accept that lack of control because it lets them back far more fast-growing companies with the same pool of money instead of betting everything on one. Asking an investor how much of a company it bought, and whether the deal came with a board seat, is a sharp way to learn how much control it actually wanted.

LEARN ONE THING

The Nasdaq falling into a correction is a good way to learn what that word actually means on Wall Street: a drop of 10 percent or more from a recent high, as opposed to a bear market, the term reserved for a 20 percent or worse decline. Corrections happen fairly often and most resolve without turning into something worse, which is why traders distinguish it from a bear market instead of treating every selloff the same way. Knowing that line is a fast way to tell whether a falling-stocks headline describes a routine wobble or something more serious.
