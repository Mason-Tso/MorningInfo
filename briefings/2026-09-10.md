LEARN ONE THING

Today's private credit story hinged on a "gate," when a fund limits how much money investors can pull out at once, even though they were promised regular withdrawals. Funds add gates so they aren't forced to dump loans at fire-sale prices just to pay investors in a hurry. Ares capping withdrawals at 5% of a fund's assets, when investors wanted more than twice that, signals real stress in an industry that boomed by lending money banks used to lend themselves. Watching whether more firms follow suit is one of the best ways to gauge how serious the slowdown really is.
