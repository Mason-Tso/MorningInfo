THE SECONDARY BRIEF

GROWTH EQUITY 101: TPG's Sumer deal above is a good way to learn continuation funds. Instead of selling a company it still believes in, a firm moves it into a brand-new fund it also manages, giving original investors a choice: cash out now, or roll their stake into the new vehicle and stay in for another run. These vehicles have become one of the hottest tools in private markets, with the industry raising well over $60 billion a year through them, because a quiet market for IPOs and big sales means firms need another way to hand cash back to investors while keeping their best companies. Asking an investor whether their firm has ever rolled a winner into a continuation fund instead of selling it outright is a sharp way to learn how patient, or how stuck, some of its best bets really are.

LEARN ONE THING

Barclays missing its bond-trading targets "by about 10 percent" is a good way to learn basis points, the tiny unit traders use instead of plain percentages. One basis point is one-hundredth of a percent, so 100 basis points equals exactly 1 percent; traders use the smaller unit because rates and yields often move by fractions of a percent that still matter enormously to prices. That is why a trader says the Fed hiked rates "25 basis points" instead of "a quarter of one percent," and why asking "how many bps did that move" is a fast way to sound like you belong on a desk.
