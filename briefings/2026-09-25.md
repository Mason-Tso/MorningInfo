GROWTH EQUITY 101: Island's round drew eight-plus funds at once, and growth investors care as much about how a return gets measured as how big it looks. MOIC [multiple on invested capital] is how many times over you got your money back, say 3x. IRR [internal rate of return] folds in how long that took, since 3x in two years beats 3x in ten. DPI [distributions to paid-in capital] counts actual cash paid back to investors, not paper value, because a fund can look great on paper for years before returning a dollar. Asking which of the three a firm cares about most is a fast way to learn how it gets judged.

LEARN ONE THING

This week's climb in the 10-year Treasury yield to its highest level since 2007 isn't just about the Fed. Investors also demand a "term premium," extra compensation for locking money up for 10 or 30 years instead of something short-term, when they're nervous about inflation or how much debt the US needs to sell. A rising term premium is why long-term rates can climb even in weeks when the Fed does nothing, part of why bond desks have been so busy lately.
