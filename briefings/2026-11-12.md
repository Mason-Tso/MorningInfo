GROWTH EQUITY 101: Sequoia's move is a good way to learn evergreen funds. A traditional fund has roughly a 10-year life: it raises money, invests it, then must sell everything and return cash to its LPs [limited partners, the pensions and endowments supplying the capital] by a deadline. An evergreen fund has no deadline: it keeps holding winners indefinitely, even public stock after an IPO, and raises fresh LP money on a rolling basis instead of one big closing. Sequoia restructured into this model years ago precisely so it never has to force-sell a great company. Asking an investor whether their money is evergreen or has a fixed life is a sharp way to learn how patient they can really afford to be.

LEARN ONE THING

The Binance hack is a good way to learn the difference between a hot wallet and a cold wallet. A hot wallet stays connected to the internet so an exchange can move funds quickly, which is exactly what makes it a target; a cold wallet is kept offline, usually on a physical device, where hackers simply cannot reach it. Exchanges keep most customer funds in cold storage and only a slice in hot wallets for liquidity, which is why Binance's $6.3 billion loss was a fraction of its total holdings. It's also why serious investors move anything they aren't actively trading off an exchange into their own cold wallet.
