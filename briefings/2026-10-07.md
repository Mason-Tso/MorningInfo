GROWTH EQUITY 101: Impulse Space's jump from $4.26 billion to $5.4 billion in the same funding round is a valuation step-up: each new slice of money prices in higher per share because the company hit milestones investors believe in. The opposite, a down round, is raising at a lower valuation than before, usually a sign growth slowed, and it stings because existing investors and employees get diluted more to raise the same cash. Founders dislike down rounds enough that some negotiate complicated share structures just to avoid the label. Asking whether a company's last round was a step-up or a down round, and by how much, quickly reveals how a deal team really feels about a business.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's Solana story mentions "delivery versus payment," or DvP: both sides of a trade, the asset and the cash, move at the exact same instant instead of on separate days. Without DvP there is "settlement risk," the chance one side sends what it owes and the other never follows through, which is why stock trades normally take two business days to clear. Blockchains can enforce DvP automatically in code, which is why banks are experimenting with them even without caring about crypto prices; it is really about faster, safer plumbing underneath conventional finance.
