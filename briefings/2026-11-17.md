GROWTH EQUITY 101: Norwest's new $4 billion fund is a good way to learn the three numbers LPs [limited partners: the pensions and endowments who supply a fund's money] check before committing to a firm's next fund. IRR, internal rate of return, measures how fast a fund grew money, accounting for how long it was tied up. MOIC, multiple on invested capital, just asks how many times over a fund turned a dollar into, ignoring time. DPI, distributions to paid-in capital, measures cash actually handed back, as opposed to gains still sitting on paper. A fund can show a great MOIC while its DPI stays near zero if it hasn't sold anything yet, exactly the gap LPs probe before re-upping.

LEARN ONE THING

Nvidia's leadership shakeup is a good way to learn why companies separate the board chair and CEO roles, something Nvidia never had until this week. The CEO runs the business day to day; the chair runs the board, the outside group that hires, fires and oversees that CEO. Naming Harvey Jones its first-ever board chair, right as it names an interim CEO, is the board asserting independent oversight after Jensen Huang held founder, CEO and chairman power all at once for over 30 years. It's a common move after a crisis, since it's hard for a board to police a CEO who also runs the meetings where that policing would happen.
