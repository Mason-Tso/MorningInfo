LEARN ONE THING

Today's Goldman numbers mention ROE and ROTE, two ways banks measure how well they use shareholders' money. ROE, or return on equity, divides profit by total shareholder equity; ROTE, return on tangible equity, strips out goodwill and other items that aren't real, usable capital, so it runs a bit higher and is seen as more honest. Goldman's roughly 19 percent ROE and 20.5 percent ROTE are both strong, since anything above about 15 percent counts as a good quarter on Wall Street. Asking which number a bank highlights in its release is a quick way to gauge how good a quarter really was.
