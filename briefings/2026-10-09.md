GROWTH EQUITY 101: EliseAI pouring its new $350 million into sales reps is a good way to learn unit economics, the math behind how a subscription company makes money. CAC [customer acquisition cost] is what it costs to land one new landlord; LTV [lifetime value] is what that landlord pays over the years they stay; "payback period" is how many months of revenue it takes to recoup the CAC. A company with a short payback period can afford to hire more salespeople, since each new customer pays for itself quickly, exactly the bet behind EliseAI's new hires. Asking an investor a company's CAC payback period is a fast way to show you understand whether growth is actually profitable.

LEARN ONE THING

This week's bitcoin dip involved "liquidations," a word that comes up constantly in crypto trading. When a trader borrows money to bet bigger than their cash allows, called trading on leverage, the exchange holds their cash as collateral. If the price moves against them enough that the collateral can't cover the loss, the exchange automatically closes the position, a liquidation, often making the move even faster as everyone gets sold out at once. That is what happened to roughly $487 million of bets this week when bitcoin briefly dropped under $84,000, and it is why leveraged crypto trading can turn a small dip into a much bigger one.
