GROWTH EQUITY 101: Most growth and venture funds are "closed-end," meaning they raise a fixed pool of money and must return it to their LPs [the pensions and endowments who supplied it] within roughly ten years. GIC, the sovereign fund behind today's Supabase round, is "evergreen": it never has to return the money and can keep reinvesting indefinitely, since it's investing a country's reserves, not a group of LPs on a deadline. That patience lets evergreen investors like sovereign funds compete directly with ten-year funds for the same hot deals. Asking an investor whether their fund is closed-end or evergreen shows you understand how much time pressure they're under.

LEARN ONE THING

The SEC just approved funds that aim to move three times as much as bitcoin or ether each day, but these "leveraged" funds reset daily, not over the life of your investment. If bitcoin rises 10% one day and falls 10% the next, a 3x fund doesn't land back where it started, it actually loses value, since each day's move compounds on a new starting point. That effect, called volatility decay, is why traders use these for short-term bets, not months-long holds, and it's worth knowing before anyone says "just triple your bitcoin exposure."
