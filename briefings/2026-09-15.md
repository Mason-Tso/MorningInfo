LEARN ONE THING

Bond prices and bond yields move in opposite directions, which is why the 10-year yield hitting its highest level since 2007 actually means bond prices are falling. Think of a bond as a fixed IOU: a $1,000 bond paying $50 a year yields 5%, but if nervous investors sell it down to $900, that same $50 payment is now a bigger share of what a new buyer paid, so the yield rises even though the bond itself never changed. Investors are selling Treasurys now because they expect a Fed hike tomorrow, and higher rates elsewhere make an old bond's fixed payment look less attractive. "Yields rising" and "prices falling" describe the same thing, and traders use the two interchangeably.
