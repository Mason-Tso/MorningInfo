GROWTH EQUITY 101: Pigment's round is a good way to learn what makes growth equity its own category, different from venture capital and from a buyout. Venture capital mostly funds young, unprofitable companies betting on an idea; a buyout firm typically buys an entire mature company and loads it with debt to run differently. Growth equity sits in between: EQT's fund bought a minority stake in Pigment, a company with real customers already, using no debt, betting the business just needs cash to grow faster. Asking an investor whether a deal used debt, and whether it bought a minority stake or full control, is a fast way to learn which of the three a firm actually does.

LEARN ONE THING

Intel and Amazon's shaky guidance this week is a good chance to learn capex, short for capital expenditure: money a company spends on physical things like chip factories or data centers that it will use for years, not a cost that's gone the moment it's spent. Wall Street now watches capex numbers almost as closely as profit, since so much of the AI boom depends on whether that spending eventually turns into revenue. When a company signals that spending needs longer to pay off, investors read it as a sign the AI cycle could be peaking, exactly what spooked the market Friday.
