GROWTH EQUITY 101: Axiom's $2 billion round is a good way to learn structured and preferred equity, what growth investors often buy instead of plain common stock. Preferred shares typically get paid back before common stockholders if a company is ever sold or wound down, and "convertible" preferred can later turn into common shares if the company does well, letting the investor have it both ways. Growth investors like this structure because it limits downside on huge checks while still sharing in the upside. Asking an investor whether their check was common or structured preferred is a sharp way to learn how much downside protection they actually built in.

LEARN ONE THING

This week's Fed meeting is a good chance to learn the "dot plot," the Fed's nickname for its Summary of Economic Projections, where each policymaker anonymously marks a dot showing where they think rates should be over the next few years. The Fed only publishes it four times a year, at the March, June, September and December meetings, so Wednesday's decision comes with no fresh dots despite being one of the most watched meetings in months. Investors instead have to read the Fed's thinking mostly from the press conference, which is why those 2:30 p.m. remarks matter more than usual this time.
