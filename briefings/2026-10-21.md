GROWTH EQUITY 101: Today's secondary-sale trend is a good way to learn primary money versus a tender offer. In a primary round, new money goes straight into the company, like Tenstorrent's or Modal's raises above. A tender offer is different: an investor buys existing shares from current holders, usually employees sitting on paper wealth, so the company never sees the cash. OpenAI and Anthropic use tender offers to let early employees cash out without going public. Asking whether a deal was new money or a purchase of existing shares is a sharp way to learn what it accomplished.

LEARN ONE THING

With the Fed, the ECB and the Bank of Japan all meeting the same week, it's worth learning the yield curve: the line connecting Treasury yields from short-term bonds, like the 2-year, to long-term ones, like the 10-year. Longer bonds normally pay more, since lenders want extra reward for tying up money longer. When short-term yields rise above long-term ones, the curve "inverts," historically one of the more reliable recession warnings, since it means investors expect the Fed to cut rates later to fight a slowdown. Watching the 2-year versus the 10-year after next week's decisions beats listening to what any one central bank says out loud.
