LEARN ONE THING

Oura had already set a price range for its IPO this week, the last step before selling shares, when it postponed the offering anyway despite saying demand was strong. Before a company prices an IPO, its bankers spend days on a "roadshow," pitching big investors and building a "book" of orders at different prices; the final price gets set from that book, not guessed in advance. When a roadshow goes well but broader markets are swinging hard, like this week's oil and yield moves, a company can still pull back rather than risk pricing into a falling market on day one.
