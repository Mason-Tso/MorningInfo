GROWTH EQUITY 101: Capitolis is a good example of why a bank's own venture arm, like Citi Ventures here, can see deals other investors can't. Citi was not just an investor, it was already a customer using Capitolis's software to manage its own trading risk, so its deal team had months of hands-on experience with the product before anyone talked valuation. That is the edge a corporate or strategic investor has over a traditional venture fund: it can judge whether something actually works inside a real business, not just whether the pitch sounds good. Asking an investor whether they were a customer before they were a shareholder is a sharp way to learn how they really got comfortable with a deal.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's jump in Fed rate-hike odds comes from somewhere specific: the futures market for the Fed's own overnight rate [the fed funds rate]. Traders buy and sell contracts betting on where that rate lands after each Fed meeting, and the price of those contracts converts into a probability, like the roughly one-in-five chance of an October hike that became almost two-in-five overnight. It's real money changing hands on a bet, not a poll, which is why "the odds just moved" counts as hard data on a trading desk.
