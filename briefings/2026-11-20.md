GROWTH EQUITY 101: Goldman Sachs Alternatives leading Payhawk's round alone is a good way to learn how a fund works. A firm like Goldman raises money from LPs [limited partners: the pensions and endowments supplying the cash], pools it into a fund, and its deal team, the GPs [general partners], decides where it goes. In exchange, GPs typically charge LPs a management fee, often around 2 percent a year, plus carry, a cut of the profits, commonly around 20 percent, once the fund returns investors' money. That is why so many people want to work at a firm like Goldman Sachs Alternatives: carry on one winning deal can dwarf a salary.

LEARN ONE THING

Nvidia trading at about 12 times next year's expected profit, the cheapest since 2012, is a good way to learn the forward price-to-earnings ratio, or forward P/E: a stock's price divided by what analysts expect it to earn per share next year, not what it already earned. A high forward P/E means investors expect fast growth; a low one, like Nvidia's right now, usually means they are worried about the future even though the company is still growing today.
