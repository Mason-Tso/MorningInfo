TECHCRUNCH

GROWTH EQUITY 101: Armadin's round is a good window into how a fund actually works. The firms writing checks, Andreessen Horowitz, Accel, Bain Capital Ventures and the rest, are General Partners, or GPs, the people who pick deals and sit on boards. The money itself mostly comes from Limited Partners, or LPs, pensions, endowments and wealthy families who are not involved in picking deals. GPs typically charge LPs a management fee of around 2 percent of the fund each year, plus carry, roughly 20 percent of any profits once the fund pays LPs back. When nine firms pile into one round like Armadin's, each is really acting on behalf of thousands of pension beneficiaries one step removed, which is why asking an investor "whose money are you actually investing" is a sharp coffee-chat question.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's Manus story is a good chance to learn about foreign-investment review, the tool governments use to block deals on national-security grounds. Chinese regulators blocked Meta's purchase of Manus, worried about AI technology flowing to a US company; America has its own version, a body called CFIUS, that reviews foreign purchases of US companies for the same reason. Both countries now treat leading AI firms like defense contractors, too strategically important to let a rival nation control, which is why big cross-border AI and chip deals increasingly die in review long before price becomes the issue.
