GROWTH EQUITY 101: Today's NexBank raise is a good example of structured equity: shares built with specific rights, or the lack of them, attached. Most growth deals use preferred stock, which gets paid back first in a sale, but a company can also sell non-voting common stock, like NexBank did, to raise money without handing over any control. Investors accept that trade-off more easily when they're buying into a stable, profitable business rather than a risky bet where they'd want more say. Asking an investor what rights came with their shares, voting power, a board seat, or neither, shows you understand how much influence a check actually buys.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's SEC proposal sits next to a law called the GENIUS Act, which drew a sharp line between two kinds of digital dollars. A stablecoin, like Circle's USDC, is a token backed by reserves that by law cannot pay you interest. A tokenized deposit, the kind big banks are building, is just a bank deposit turned into a movable digital token, so it keeps paying interest and keeps its FDIC insurance, since it's still a liability of the bank. That's why banks keep racing to build tokenized deposits instead of using existing stablecoins: they keep both the interest income and the deposit relationship.
