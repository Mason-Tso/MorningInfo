GROWTH EQUITY 101: Isomorphic Labs going from a $2.1 billion valuation in May to a reported $50 billion six months later is a good way to learn the valuation step-up: each new round prices a company higher than the last, usually because it hit a milestone investors believe in. The opposite, a down round, happens when a company raises at a lower price than before, a sign investors think it's worth less than they used to. Step-ups are the norm in a hot market like AI right now; down rounds are rare in good times but spread fast once a sector cools, which is why investors watch them as a temperature check on an industry. Asking an investor whether their last few deals were step-ups or down rounds is a sharp way to learn how hot they think the market really is.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's delayed inflation report is a good way to learn why the Fed watches a different number than the CPI everyone hears about. Its preferred gauge, core PCE, tracks what people actually buy as they switch between products, like swapping steak for chicken when beef gets pricier, while CPI tracks a fixed basket and tends to run hotter. That's why the two reports can tell slightly different stories, and why traders treat PCE days as the bigger event.
