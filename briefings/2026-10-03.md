GROWTH EQUITY 101: When Sequoia writes a check into a company like Kalshi, it isn't their own money; most comes from pension funds, endowments and wealthy families, called limited partners, or LPs, who hand cash to the firm, called the general partner, or GP, to invest for them. The GP charges LPs a management fee, commonly around 2% of the fund's money each year to run the firm, plus carry, a cut of the profits, often 20%, once the fund returns money. A round like Kalshi's is really a GP convincing its LPs the bet is worth making. Asking an investor how their fund splits fee versus carry shows you understand how the firm itself makes money.

LEARN ONE THING

When people say bank stocks fell into a "correction," they mean the group is down at least 10% from its recent peak; a "bear market" is the harsher label, for a drop of 20% or more. Big bank stocks have fallen about 14% since August, enough to count as a correction but nowhere near a bear market, even as the broader market sits at records. That gap is normal: it means investors see risk concentrated in one place, like high rates squeezing bank profits, not spread across the whole economy.
