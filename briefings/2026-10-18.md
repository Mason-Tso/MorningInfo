LEARN ONE THING

Today's UK budget story is a good chance to learn about a bank surcharge, an extra tax some governments charge only on bank profits, on top of the normal corporate tax every company pays. The UK cut its surcharge to 3 percent from 8 percent a few years ago to stay competitive, and now reportedly wants to push it back up to raise money without touching income tax. Governments like taxing bank profits because banks are hard to move overseas and look like an easy target right after a strong earnings season, exactly the spot UK banks are in now. That makes a surcharge different from a one-time "windfall tax": it's permanent, built into every budget from here on.
