LEARN ONE THING

AMD is buying World Labs entirely in stock, not cash, what bankers call an "all-stock deal." Instead of getting paid in dollars, World Labs' investors and employees get shares of AMD itself, so their payout rises or falls with AMD's stock price before the deal closes. Companies use all-stock deals to conserve cash or when they believe their own stock is strong currency; sellers accept it when they believe in the buyer's future or have no better cash offer. It's why an "$8.2 billion deal" can end up worth more or less later, since the number moves with the acquirer's stock.
