GROWTHLIST

GROWTH EQUITY 101: Model ML's jump to a $1.5 billion valuation is a good way to learn the Rule of 40, a quick gut-check growth investors use on software and AI companies. Add a company's revenue growth rate to its profit margin; if the two together clear 40, the business is considered healthy, whether it gets there by growing fast while losing money or growing slower while turning a solid profit. A company well below 40 on both counts is a much harder sell, no matter how exciting its technology sounds. Asking an investor "does this one clear the Rule of 40" is a fast way to sound like you know how growth firms screen deals.

LEARN ONE THING

Anthropic's stock opening 34 percent above its $83 IPO price, then sliding below $100 within hours, is a good way to learn what a "pop" is. Banks deliberately price an IPO below what they think public investors will pay, leaving room for the stock to jump on its first trade, a reward for early investors and a marketing moment for the company. The catch: a huge pop one day doesn't guarantee the stock holds those gains, which is why bankers watch where a stock settles days later, not just where it opens.
