COMPANY

GROWTH EQUITY 101: Today's two rounds are a good study in the difference between venture capital and growth equity, terms Mason will hear used almost interchangeably but that mean different bets. Venture investors like Andreessen Horowitz, writing TypeSafe's Series A, bet on a team and an idea before there's much revenue to check, which is why a three-week-old company can be worth $7.5 billion. Growth equity investors instead wait for proven, growing revenue, then pay for a minority stake to help a company scale, closer to the bet behind Oxide's backlog-driven round. A third category, buyout firms, buys whole companies outright with borrowed money and takes over decisions completely. Asking an investor which of the three describes their firm is a fast way to learn how they spend their days.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's New York Life item mentions a "forward-flow" deal, a structure common in consumer and private credit: an investor like an insurer agrees upfront to keep buying a lender's new loans, month after month, rather than a pile of existing ones at once. That gives the lender predictable funding and gives the investor steady interest income without running the lending business itself, which is why insurers have become reliable backstops for consumer and auto lenders.
