BLOOMBERG

GROWTH EQUITY 101: General Atlantic's own IPO push is worth knowing: growth-equity firms increasingly go public or sell stakes in themselves, not just in the companies they back. Going public gives a firm a permanent pool of capital instead of raising a new fund every few years, and lets founders cash out some ownership without losing control. It also signals a firm wants steadier, recurring fee income rather than depending purely on unpredictable investment profits. Blackstone, KKR and Apollo all did this years ago and are now some of the largest public companies in finance, exactly the playbook General Atlantic is following.

LEARN ONE THING

Nscale's giant pre-IPO raise came as convertible financing, meaning investors gave the company cash today for a note that converts into stock later, often at a discount to whatever price the IPO eventually sets. Companies use converts when they want money fast without agreeing on a hard valuation now, and investors like them because they get downside protection: if the IPO flops, they're often still owed their money back like a bond.
