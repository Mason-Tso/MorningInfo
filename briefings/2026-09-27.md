GROWTH EQUITY 101: Precision Neuroscience is pre-revenue and years from FDA approval, exactly when a term called "liquidation preference" matters most. It's a right written into preferred shares, the type growth investors almost always buy, guaranteeing their money back first if the company is ever sold or shut down, before common shareholders, usually founders and employees, see a dollar. A "1x" preference just returns the original check; some deals ask for more. It's how growth investors protect a risky, pre-revenue bet, and asking whether a deal was "1x non-participating" or more aggressive is a fast way to sound like you understand deal terms.

LEARN ONE THING

Oil near $93 a barrel isn't just about how much crude the world pumps and burns; a chunk of that price is a "risk premium," extra cost investors pay in case the war disrupts supply through the Strait of Hormuz, even though oil is still flowing near normal volumes today. That's what JPMorgan meant by having no baseline forecast anymore: an economist can normally model supply and demand, but a premium driven by whether Iran and the US strike each other next week is closer to a bet on politics than economics. It's why oil can jump or drop sharply on a single headline, like Trump's rejection this weekend, without a barrel actually changing hands differently.
