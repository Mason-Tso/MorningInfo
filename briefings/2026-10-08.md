GROWTH EQUITY 101: TXSE is a good way to understand "take rate," the small cut a platform keeps from money flowing through it. An exchange earns a tiny fee on every share traded, trivial-sounding until multiplied by billions of shares a day, which is why banks will bankroll a new entrant: owning a piece of TXSE means capturing that fee stream instead of paying it to NYSE and Nasdaq forever. Payment networks like Visa and exchanges like Coinbase run on the same logic. Asking an investor "what's the take rate, and who pays it" is a fast way to show you understand how a platform business makes money.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's Fed story uses two words that come up constantly on trading desks: "hawks" and "doves." A hawk leans toward raising rates to fight inflation, even at the cost of slower growth; a dove prefers lower rates, even if it risks more inflation. In yesterday's minutes, Logan played the hawk and Williams played the dove. Knowing who leans which way is how traders turn one Fed meeting into a story about where rates go next.
