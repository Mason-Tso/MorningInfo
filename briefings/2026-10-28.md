GROWTH EQUITY 101: Today's "forever fund" pitch from Goldman and Evercore is a good way to learn what an evergreen fund actually is. A normal private-equity or growth fund has a fixed life, usually around ten years: raise money, invest it, sell the companies, and return the cash to LPs [limited partners, the pensions and endowments supplying the capital]. An evergreen fund never has to wind down; it can hold a winning company for 15 years if it wants, and reinvest sale proceeds instead of handing them back. Firms like these because a ten-year clock sometimes forces them to sell a great company before it's done growing. Asking an investor whether their fund has a fixed life or is evergreen is a sharp way to learn how patient their money really is.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's Fed meeting is a good chance to learn the Fed's "dual mandate," the two goals Congress legally requires it to balance at once: stable prices (low inflation) and maximum employment. Those goals can pull in opposite directions, which is exactly today's dilemma, since inflation is still running above the Fed's target while a recent weak jobs report showed hiring nearly stalling. Raising rates fights inflation but risks slowing hiring further, while holding steady risks letting inflation linger, which is why a decision that sounds simple ("hold" or "hike") actually reflects a genuine tug-of-war between the two goals.
