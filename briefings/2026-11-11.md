LEARN ONE THING

Tuesday's market is a good way to learn market breadth: how many stocks in an index are actually rising, not just how the index itself moves. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq both jumped even though Nvidia, one of the biggest stocks in both, fell again, because enough other chip and tech stocks rose even more to outweigh it. Traders watch breadth because a rally built on hundreds of stocks rising is considered healthier than one propped up by a handful of giant names. The last few weeks have been a real-time lesson in both versions.
