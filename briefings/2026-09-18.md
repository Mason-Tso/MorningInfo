GROWTH EQUITY 101: Most growth funds have a fixed lifespan, usually around ten years, after which the firm must return investors' money and raise a brand new fund. An evergreen fund like the one General Atlantic just launched skips that clock: it never closes, keeps reinvesting profits, and lets investors buy in or cash out periodically instead of waiting a decade. That suits patient capital, like wealthy individuals who don't need money back on a schedule, and gives the firm steadier fee income instead of the boom-and-bust cycle of raising a new multi-billion-dollar fund every few years. Mentioning evergreen funds in a coffee chat is a good way to ask how capital structure shapes a firm's investing style.

LEARN ONE THING

Today is a "quadruple witching" day, when stock options, index options, index futures and single-stock futures all expire in the same session, something that happens only four times a year. Traders who used those contracts to bet on or hedge against price moves have to close out or roll their positions before the close, which can cause a burst of unusually heavy trading volume. Worth knowing before reading too much into any single day's price swings, since some of the move is mechanical, not news-driven.
