GROWTH EQUITY 101: Anthropic's lockup release above is a good way to learn secondaries and tender offers, two ways early investors get cash without waiting for a sale or an IPO. A secondary sale is just an existing shareholder selling shares to a new buyer on the side; a tender offer is a company-organized version, inviting a group of shareholders to sell a set amount at a set price, all at once. Anthropic's case is a third version, a lockup release, where the IPO paperwork itself sets dates after which insiders can sell publicly. Asking an investor whether a deal was a tender offer or just an expired lockup is a sharp way to learn how an early backer gets paid.

LEARN ONE THING

The jump in insurance costs on Oracle's and CoreWeave's debt this week is a good way to learn what a credit default swap, or CDS, actually is. It works like insurance: an investor pays a regular fee to a counterparty, and if the company whose debt is covered defaults, the counterparty pays out. When that fee rises sharply, as it has for AI data-center debt, it means more investors are paying up to protect against a default, even if none thinks one is likely yet. Traders watch CDS spreads as an early-warning gauge because they tend to move before stock prices admit anything is wrong.
