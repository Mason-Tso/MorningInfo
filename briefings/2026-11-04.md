GROWTH EQUITY 101: Ramp's round is a good way to learn how a growth fund like Dragoneer actually works. A fund raises money from LPs [limited partners: the pensions, endowments and wealthy families supplying the capital], who hand their cash to the GP [general partner, the firm itself] to invest over many years. The GP charges a management fee, typically around 2 percent of the fund a year, just to keep the lights on, then keeps carry [carried interest], usually about 20 percent of any profit, once it actually returns money. That structure is why GPs get paid to find winners like Ramp, not just to collect assets; asking an investor how their fund's fees and carry work is a fast way to learn what motivates them.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's election is a good chance to learn about a "lame duck" session, the stretch between an election and when newly elected lawmakers take their seats. The outgoing Congress, even the party that just lost its majority, keeps full power to pass laws until the new one is sworn in on January 3rd, which is why fights over government funding often get crammed into these final weeks. It is called "lame duck" because departing lawmakers have little left to lose politically, which can make them easier to deal with or harder to predict. Expect to hear the phrase a lot before January.
