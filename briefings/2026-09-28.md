GROWTH EQUITY 101: Today's Ema round shows why a growth firm like Creaegis buys a minority stake instead of the whole company like a buyout firm would. A minority stake is usually 10% to 40%, enough for real ownership and often a board seat, without taking over decisions from the founders who built the business. Growth investors want that because they're betting on a founding team's ability to keep executing a plan that already works, not their own ability to run it better, and it spares the company the debt a buyout usually requires. Asking why a firm would rather own 20% of something working than 100% of something it would have to fix is a good coffee-chat question.

LEARN ONE THING

Gold usually rises when investors are scared, but today it fell even as tensions with Iran worsened, because gold competes with bonds for the same nervous money. Treasury yields near 5% mean investors can now earn a solid, safe return just by holding government debt, while gold pays no interest at all, so when yields rise fast, safe-haven money shifts out of gold into bonds and the dollar instead. That "opportunity cost" of holding a non-interest-paying asset is why gold and yields often move in opposite directions.
