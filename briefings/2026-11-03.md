GROWTH EQUITY 101: Kalshi's price tag is a good way to learn how growth deals get priced off ARR, or annual recurring revenue [a company's current pace of sales, annualized]. Instead of valuing a company on profit the way public markets often do, growth investors multiply its ARR by a number, like Kalshi's roughly 10x, reflecting how fast and reliably that revenue is expected to keep growing. A company growing ARR 100 percent a year can command a far richer multiple than one growing slowly, which is why two companies with identical revenue today can get wildly different price tags. Asking an investor what multiple of ARR they paid is a sharp way to learn how much growth they're underwriting.

It comes from how expensive options on a stock get right before the event: if traders pay a lot for the right to buy or sell at a set price, that signals a big move is expected, though the options market never says which direction.
