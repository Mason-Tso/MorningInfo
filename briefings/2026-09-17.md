GROWTH EQUITY 101: Vista Equity Partners, which led Arcee's round, usually does buyouts, buying a whole software company and running it. Its Arcee deal instead looks like classic growth equity: a minority stake, no control, in a company that just needs cash to keep scaling fast. The difference matters because a growth investor is betting purely on the company executing its own plan, while a buyout investor is also betting on its own ability to run the business better. A buyout firm like Vista writing a growth-style minority check is often a sign it sees more upside in fast growth than in operational fixes, worth asking about in a coffee chat.

LEARN ONE THING

Yesterday's Fed decision was a "25 basis point" hike, and a basis point is just one-hundredth of a percentage point, so 25 of them equal a quarter of one percent. Traders use basis points instead of percentages because rate moves are usually small, and "25 basis points" is less confusing than saying "0.25 percentage points" out loud on a trading floor. Next time you hear a rate move described in "bps" (pronounced "bips"), remember: 100 basis points always equals 1 percentage point.
