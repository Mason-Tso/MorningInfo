GROWTH EQUITY 101: When General Atlantic sells Gen II after six years for a reported $5.1 billion, three numbers measure how good that bet was. MOIC [multiple on invested capital] is simplest: how many times the money came back, ignoring how long it took. IRR [internal rate of return] adjusts for time, so doubling your money in two years beats doubling it in ten. DPI [distributed to paid-in] only counts cash actually paid out, not a paper gain still on the books, which is why LPs trust it most. Asking whether a deal was a good MOIC but a mediocre IRR shows you know the difference.

LEARN ONE THING

This week's SpaceX news is a good excuse to learn what "spectrum" means. Every wireless signal, a call, a text, a video stream, travels over a slice of the radio-frequency airwaves, and the government controls who can use which slice so networks don't interfere. Companies either win that right at a government auction or buy it from someone who already holds it, which is what SpaceX just did, and owning enough of it is what lets a company call itself a carrier instead of just an app. That's why a spectrum deal moves stocks even though no physical product changes hands.
