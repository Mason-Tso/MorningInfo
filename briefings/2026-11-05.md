GROWTH EQUITY 101: Anduril's tender is a good moment to notice something else in growth investing: more of the firms that back companies like Anduril are themselves going public, instead of just raising a fresh fund from LPs [limited partners, the pensions and endowments that supply the money] every few years. Blue Owl, Apollo, Ares and KKR all already trade as public stocks, which gives them permanent capital instead of having to ask investors for a brand-new commitment every decade. The trade-off: permanent capital means never fundraising again, but it brings quarterly earnings calls and public shareholders demanding steady profit growth, pressure a normal ten-year fund never faces. Asking an investor whether their own firm is public, and why, is a good way to learn how much they value flexibility over permanent capital.

LEARN ONE THING

Yesterday's market had an odd wrinkle worth understanding: stocks surged, but the 10-year Treasury yield, the benchmark rate feeding into mortgages and car loans, ticked up instead of down. Bond yields move for two different reasons: fear of inflation pushes them up, but so does optimism about growth, since investors demand more return once they expect a stronger economy. A decisive election result that removes uncertainty can nudge yields higher for that second, more benign reason, which is why "yields rose" is not automatically bad news.
