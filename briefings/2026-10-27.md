GROWTH EQUITY 101: Anduril's tender offer is a good way to learn IRR, MOIC and DPI, three ways funds measure how they're doing. MOIC just compares what a fund put in to what its stake is worth now, often only on paper. IRR factors in how long the money's been tied up, since doubling your money in one year beats doubling it in five. DPI, distributions to paid-in capital, is the strictest: it only counts cash actually returned to investors, not an unrealized valuation. When a fund that backed Anduril early sells some of its stake into a tender like this one, it turns a paper gain into real DPI, exactly why these tenders have become so common while IPOs stay slow. Asking an investor how their fund's DPI compares with its MOIC is a sharp way to learn how much of a winning bet is real yet.

LEARN ONE THING

Today's Galaxy Digital swing from an $85 million loss last quarter to a $323 million profit this quarter is a good chance to learn mark-to-market accounting. Companies that hold bitcoin, ether or other crypto on their books have to record its current market value every quarter, not just what they originally paid, so a swing in crypto prices shows up directly in earnings the way it wouldn't for a company holding something like a factory. That's why crypto-exposed firms post such huge income swings quarter to quarter even when the underlying business barely changed. In a coffee chat, pointing out that a crypto company's profit is often just the token market's recent mood, not operating performance, shows you understand why those earnings are so volatile.
