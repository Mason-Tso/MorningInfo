GROWTH EQUITY 101: Vista's possible sale of Jamf is a good way to learn the two main ways a private company's backers cash out: an IPO or an acquisition. In an IPO, the company sells shares to public investors, which takes months of preparation and leaves the price exposed to whatever the market is doing that week, as Anthropic is learning right now. An acquisition is faster: another company or firm buys the whole business in one negotiated deal, with the price locked the moment both sides sign. Firms take whichever route pays more at the time, which is why the same company can spend years being pitched for an IPO and then suddenly get sold instead.

LEARN ONE THING

Friday's jump in the 10-year Treasury yield is a good way to learn the Fed does not directly control it. The Fed only sets short-term rates, the overnight cost of money for banks; the 10-year is set by investors trading bonds every day on their own guess about inflation and growth years out. That is why it could jump above 5% on one hot inflation report even though the Fed has not moved, and why mortgage rates, which track the 10-year more than the Fed's own rate, can rise even when the Fed stands still.
