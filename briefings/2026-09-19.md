GROWTH EQUITY 101: A common yardstick growth investors use is the Rule of 40: add a software company's revenue growth rate to its profit margin, and the total should be at least 40. A company growing 60% a year but losing money can still pass if its losses aren't too deep, while a slower-growing company must be far more profitable to clear the same bar. Thatch's revenue growing almost sevenfold this year is exactly the kind of number that lets a young, unprofitable company pass the Rule of 40 with room to spare, part of why investors paid up for a $1 billion valuation. Asking a growth investor how a company scores on the Rule of 40 is a fast way to sound like you know how deals actually get evaluated.

LEARN ONE THING

This week showed how oil, bond yields and even bitcoin are linked through inflation expectations. When oil prices fall, investors worry less that inflation will stay high, so they accept lower yields on bonds, which is why Treasury yields eased alongside oil. Lower yields also make it easier for investors to take risks on stocks and bitcoin instead of parking money in bonds, which helps explain why bitcoin jumped the same days oil fell. It's one chain reaction, not three coincidences.
