GROWTH EQUITY 101: Bedrock's round above is a good way to learn why growth investors almost always buy a minority stake instead of the whole company, unlike a buyout firm. CapitalG and Emergence bought a slice of Bedrock, but founder Boris Sofman still runs it exactly as before, same team, same board influence. That's deliberate: a growth investor's bet is almost entirely on a founder's ability to keep executing fast, not on their own ability to run the business, which is the buyout playbook instead. Asking an investor why they didn't just buy the whole company if they liked it that much is a sharp way to learn how much they actually trust the people running it.

LEARN ONE THING

Anthropic's delayed IPO timeline is a good chance to learn about a confidential filing, paperwork a company preparing to go public can submit privately to the SEC instead of right away in public. A law called the JOBS Act lets younger or smaller companies do this so competitors and the press can't see the numbers while bankers prepare, but it comes with a catch: the filing must go fully public at least 15 days before the roadshow, the string of investor meetings where the company pitches the stock, can begin. That countdown is the actual reason Anthropic's marketing can't start before November 10, even though everyone wants to move faster.
