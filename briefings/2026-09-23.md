GROWTH EQUITY 101: Cyera's new money from Goldman is a primary raise: the company issues brand-new shares and pockets the cash to grow. A secondary sale or tender offer is different, existing shareholders, like early employees, sell already-issued shares to someone else, and the company gets none of that money. Growth companies use primary rounds when they need fresh capital, and secondaries when early holders just want to cash out paper wealth without forcing a sale or IPO. Asking a growth investor whether a deal was primary, secondary, or a mix is a fast way to learn what the money was actually for.

LEARN ONE THING

When Citigroup sold $12 billion of bonds this week, it received about $40 billion in orders, more than three times what it was selling, something bankers call being "oversubscribed." A heavily oversubscribed deal lets the seller lower the rate it pays, since so many buyers are competing for a limited number of bonds, the same way a popular apartment listing lets a landlord raise the rent. That's why big banks sell bonds right after calming news, when demand is strongest. Next time you read a deal was "oversubscribed," it usually means the seller borrowed more cheaply than expected.
