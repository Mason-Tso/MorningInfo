GROWTH EQUITY 101: General Compute's single $100 million customer contract is a good way to learn unit economics, the math investors use to judge whether a company makes money on each customer it adds, not just in total. Three numbers matter most: CAC [cost to land a customer], LTV [lifetime value, how much revenue that customer brings in over time], and payback period, how long it takes to earn back what was spent winning them. A $100 million-a-year customer that cost little to acquire is about as good as unit economics get, which is why a one-year-old company got valued at $800 million. Asking a growth investor what a company's payback period looks like is a sharp way to learn whether its growth is actually profitable.

LEARN ONE THING

Yesterday's 10-2 Fed vote is a good chance to learn that Fed decisions aren't always unanimous. The Federal Open Market Committee [the Fed's rate-setting group] has twelve voting members, and when two dissent, it signals how split policymakers really are behind closed doors. A unanimous vote says the Fed is confident in its call; a split vote says watch the next meeting closely, since the dissenters are already pushing for change.
