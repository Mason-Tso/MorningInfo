GROWTH EQUITY 101: Anduril's deal above is a tender offer: insiders selling shares they already own for cash, while the company itself sees none of the money. A continuation fund solves a different problem for the same kind of firm. Instead of insiders cashing out, the fund itself rolls its stake in a company it loves but isn't ready to sell into a brand-new vehicle it also manages, giving its original investors a choice: take their money out now, or roll it into the new fund and stay in for another run. These vehicles have become one of the hottest tools in private markets, raising more than $60 billion industry-wide last year, precisely because fewer IPOs and big sales mean firms need another way to return cash to investors while still holding onto their best companies. Asking an investor whether their firm has ever rolled a winner into a continuation fund is a sharp way to learn how patient, or how stuck, some of its best bets really are.

LEARN ONE THING

Robinhood's numbers this past week are a good chance to learn what a prediction market, or event contract, actually is: a bet structured like a financial contract, where you buy a "yes" or "no" position on something like an election or an economic report, and it pays out a fixed amount if you're right and nothing if you're wrong. These have exploded because the CFTC, the federal commodities regulator, treats them as derivatives rather than as gambling, which lets platforms offer them nationwide instead of state by state like traditional sports betting. That regulatory gap is exactly what casinos and some state regulators are fighting to close right now. It is worth knowing heading into Tuesday, since prediction-market odds will be everywhere in the election coverage.
